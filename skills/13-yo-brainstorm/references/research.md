# Research notes: creative exploration

Use these sources to explain inspiration and limitations. Do not call this skill validated.

- **Zabelina & Robinson (2010), Child's Play.** In a randomized study of 76 undergraduates, imagining being seven years old preceded more original responses on a creativity task. This concerns a brief human priming exercise; it does not establish effects for LLMs, thirteen-year-olds or real-world business success. [Paper, DOI 10.1037/a0015644](https://www.psychologytoday.com/sites/default/files/attachments/34246/zabelina-robinson-2010a.pdf).
- **Saretzki et al. (2026), Are children really more creative than adults?** A comparison of 92 preschool children and 70 adults found higher adult creative performance, while children generally reported more positive engagement and confidence. This motivates preserving expertise alongside playfulness, but does not causally validate that combination. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0346553).
- **Zheng et al., Take a Step Back (ICLR 2024; preprint 2023).** Tested abstraction before reasoning on then-current LLMs and several benchmarks. Use the idea of returning to relevant principles without assuming the same gains on current models or all tasks. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06117).
- **Milička et al. (2024), LLMs downplay cognitive abilities to fit personas.** GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 simulated children aged one to six in false-belief tasks. This is a reason to preserve full model capability, not proof that a thirteen-year-old persona reduces performance. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0298522).

Treat option counts, staged exploration and short prototype budgets as authored workflow choices. Test their usefulness rather than attributing them to these papers.
